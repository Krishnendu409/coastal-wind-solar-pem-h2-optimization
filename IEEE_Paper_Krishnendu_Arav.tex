\begin{abstract}
Decarbonizing heavy coastal industries---such as petroleum refining, nitrogenous fertilizers, and port logistics in southwestern India---requires continuous, cost-effective green hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) supply under severe seasonal weather variability. In this work, we investigate a $500\text{ kg H}_2/\text{h}$ ($4,355\text{ t/yr}$) hybrid wind--solar proton exchange membrane water electrolysis (PEMWE) facility through an audited, multi-year Process Systems Engineering (PSE) and physics-guided machine learning framework. First, analysis of six contiguous years ($2019$--$2024$, $52,608$ hourly observations) of uninflated NASA POWER MERRA-2 meteorological data demonstrates that local coastal wind in the Udupi--Mangalore corridor ($13.35^\circ\text{N}, 74.79^\circ\text{E}$) yields a low capacity factor ($\overline{\text{CF}} = 14.70\%$), whereas open-access wheeled wind from the Chitradurga--Gadag highland corridor ($14.23^\circ\text{N}, 76.40^\circ\text{E}$, delivered $\overline{\text{CF}} = 43.29\%$ using an IEC Class IIIB low-wind turbine with $v_r = 8.4\text{ m/s}$ vs.\ $31.2\%$ for standard Class II) achieves strong seasonal complementarity with on-site coastal solar PV ($\overline{\text{CF}} = 21.23\%$). Second, a first-principles high-current PEMWE model calibrated against a literature-derived semi-empirical benchmark ($14$ points based on Bernt \& Gasteiger 2016 and Rakousky et al. 2016, Nafion 115, $75^\circ\text{C}$, $30\text{ bar}$, $\text{RMSE} = 3.10\text{ mV}$, $R^2 = 0.99971$) is coupled with catalytic recombination to guarantee anodic $\text{H}_2$-in-$\text{O}_2 \le 1.64\text{ mol\%}$ ($<50\%$ of lower explosion limit) at turndown ($i_{\min} = 0.20\text{ A/cm}^2$). Third, a strictly leak-free $2 \times 5$ surrogate ablation establishes that a compact physics-guided Multi-Layer Perceptron (\textbf{Green-MLP}, $4,612$ parameters) achieves $5$-fold cross-validation $R^2 = 0.9943 \pm 0.0031$ ($R^2 = 0.9982$ on LCOH, $\text{RMSE} = \text{INR } 1.66 \pm 0.51/\text{kg}$) with an $89.94\%$ FLOP reduction over regularized deep networks and a $4,206\times$ inference acceleration over Numba-accelerated $52,608$-hour simulations. Fourth, a $5$-seed constrained NSGA-II optimization with split-CAPEX modeling reveals a critical trade-off: dynamic current overload ($i_{\max} > 1.80\text{ A/cm}^2$) does not decrease Levelized Cost of Hydrogen (LCOH) because balance-of-plant (BOP) and rectifier capital costs scale with peak megawatt capacity, favoring a knee-point design ($48.02\text{ MW}$ wind, $72.77\text{ MW}$ solar, $43.61\text{ MW}$ nominal PEM at $i_{\max}^* = 1.764\text{ A/cm}^2$, $86.86\text{ MWh}$ battery) delivering $\text{LCOH} = \text{INR } 332.51/\text{kg H}_2$ (\$$3.96/\text{kg}$), $\text{LPSP} = 0.62\%$, and $12.0$-year stack lifetime. Wheeled highland wind lowers LCOH by $12.96\%$ relative to an optimized pure coastal hybrid plant ($\text{INR } 382.04/\text{kg}$) and avoids severe supply failure ($\text{LPSP} = 21.30\%$) if identical hardware operates solely on coastal wind. Finally, a $9$-sensor latent-variable Principal Component Analysis (PCA, $k=3$, $96.03\%$ variance) benchmarked across $100$ dynamic realizations demonstrates that while load-compensated 1D voltage-residual EWMA detects incipient membrane dehydration with marginally lower latency ($10.6\text{ h}$ vs.\ $11.6\text{ h}$), PCA provides unique multi-sensor root-cause diagnosis via contribution plots, isolating thermal-pressure anomalies ($95.2\%$ combined contribution) during dehydration and overpotential-production anomalies during anodic flooding ($100\%$ detection rate).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} Government of India's National Green Hydrogen Mission mandates the production of $5\text{ MMT/yr}$ of green hydrogen by 2030 to substitute fossil-derived grey hydrogen across petroleum refining, ammonia-based nitrogenous fertilizers, and steelmaking \cite{mnre2023}. Heavy chemical complexes in coastal Karnataka---such as Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL) and Mangalore Chemicals \& Fertilizers (MCF)---represent substantial concentrated hydrogen demands ($>100\text{ t/day}$). Siting green hydrogen facilities within coastal industrial corridors offers immediate proximity to off-takers, port bunkering terminals, and abundant seawater desalination potential \cite{pujari2026}. 

However, powering large-scale water electrolysis exclusively from on-site variable renewable energy (VRE) encounters severe techno-economic hurdles in tropical coastal regions. Tropical monsoon climates experience extreme seasonal weather shifts: solar irradiance drops precipitously during the four-month Southwest Monsoon (June--September) under dense cloud cover, whereas coastal surface winds often remain inadequate to sustain baseload multi-megawatt electrolysis throughout the remainder of the year \cite{abhishek2025, mitra2025book}. 

Recent chemical engineering and energy systems literature has focused on three core engineering strategies to address these challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{High-Current-Density Proton Exchange Membrane Water Electrolysis (PEMWE):} Operating PEMWE stacks at elevated current densities ($i > 1.8\text{ A/cm}^2$, up to $3.5\text{ A/cm}^2$) increases hydrogen generation per unit active cell area, theoretically reducing stack capital expenditure (CAPEX) \cite{masampally2026}. However, high-current operation intensifies two-phase oxygen bubble crowding within the porous transport layer (PTL), accelerates membrane-electrode assembly (MEA) ohmic dissipation, and exacerbates catalyst dissolution under dynamic load ramping \cite{carmo2013, bernt2016, rakousky2016}. Moreover, safe turndown is constrained by pressurized hydrogen-in-oxygen gas crossover, which risks exceeding the lower explosion limit ($4.0\text{ mol\%}$) at low current densities \cite{trinke2018}.
    \item \textit{Physics-Guided Surrogate-Assisted Optimization:} Sizing multi-carrier energy systems across multi-year meteorological time series ($>50,000$ hourly steps) requires tens of thousands of dynamic dispatch evaluations during evolutionary optimization \cite{inapakurthi2025}. While deep neural networks have been applied as surrogate models, unconstrained black-box networks often violate physical laws, require massive parameter counts, and risk severe training energy footprints \cite{schwartz2020}. Domain-informed ``Green AI'' architectures that enforce physical scaling laws offer a path toward computationally lean, leak-free design optimization \cite{pujari2026, raissi2019}.
    \item \textit{Multivariate Statistical Process Monitoring (MSPM):} Under fluctuating renewable power inputs, standard univariate threshold alarms (e.g., cell voltage limits) fail because operational voltages dynamically vary with current density \cite{qin2012}. Latent-variable statistical techniques, pioneered in chemical process monitoring by Jackson and Mudholkar \cite{jackson1979} and extended to nonlinear energy systems by Madakyaru and co-workers \cite{madakyaru2024, nounou2012}, utilize Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with Hotelling's $T^2$ and Squared Prediction Error ($\text{SPE}/Q$) to decouple normal load swings from internal electrochemical faults such as membrane dehydration and PTL flooding.
\end{enumerate}

Despite these individual developments, previous studies have suffered from significant critical limitations. First, simulation studies frequently evaluate green hydrogen plants over a single synthesized reference year, obscuring inter-annual climate variability and extreme seasonal droughts. Second, many models rely on uncalibrated polarization equations or omit balance-of-plant (BOP) cost scaling under peak current overloads. Third, surrogate models in prior literature have occasionally suffered from target leakage (injecting economic weighting directly into input features), confounding the true scientific value of physics guidance. Fourth, process monitoring benchmarks have often evaluated fault detection against uncompensated static voltage thresholds rather than realistic load-compensated dynamical baselines.

This paper addresses these issues by formulating and testing three central research questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Electrochemical and Regional Economic Trade-off):} How does dynamic high-current overload ($i_{\max} > 1.80\text{ A/cm}^2$) impact the Levelized Cost of Hydrogen (LCOH) when power electronics and BOP capital costs are rigorously scaled with peak capacity, and how does uninflated on-site coastal wind compare against open-access wheeled highland wind across a 6-year meteorological record?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Surrogate Generalization and Computational Efficiency):} Can a strictly leak-free, physics-guided compact neural surrogate preserve Pareto ranking ($\rho > 0.99$) and accelerate 6-year multi-objective evolutionary optimization without over-parameterized deep neural networks?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Multivariate Process Monitoring Benchmark):} Under realistic renewable dispatch trajectories, does latent-variable PCA provide an early warning lead time or superior diagnostic root-cause isolation compared to a load-compensated 1D voltage-residual EWMA control chart across repeated Monte Carlo fault realizations?
\end{itemize}

\section{Regional Meteorology, Siting, and Calibrated First-Principles Electrochemistry}

\subsection{Multi-Year Meteorological Analysis and Regional Siting}
To avoid the risks of single-year climatological anomalies, hourly meteorological data spanning six complete years ($2019$--$2024$, $N = 52,608\text{ h}$) were extracted from the NASA Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources (POWER) MERRA-2 assimilation dataset \cite{nasapower2026}. We examine two distinct geographic corridors within Karnataka, India:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Corridor 1 (On-Site Coastal Industrial Corridor):} Located at the coastal plain of Udupi--Manipal--Mangalore ($13.3525^\circ\text{N}, 74.7928^\circ\text{E}$), adjacent to industrial off-takers. Across the 6-year baseline, the mean global horizontal irradiance (GHI) is $213.92\text{ W/m}^2$. Applying an isotropic Liu--Jordan transposition model for fixed latitude-tilt arrays ($\beta = 13.4^\circ\text{S}$) with ground albedo $\rho_g = 0.20$ yields an annual mean plane-of-array (POA) irradiance of $G_{\text{poa}} = 220.59\text{ W/m}^2$, corresponding to an annual solar PV capacity factor of $\overline{\text{CF}}_{\text{solar}} = 21.23\%$ (summer peak of $24.82\%$). However, raw $100\text{ m}$ hub-height wind speed (evaluated using hourly dynamic power-law shear $\alpha(t) = \ln[v_{50}(t)/v_{10}(t)]/\ln(50/10)$ without empirical multiplier, $f_{\text{wind}} = 1.0$) averages only $4.15\text{ m/s}$, delivering an annual wind capacity factor of $\overline{\text{CF}}_{\text{wind,coastal}} = 14.70\%$ for an IEC Class IIIB turbine ($v_{\text{ci}} = 2.5\text{ m/s}, v_r = 8.4\text{ m/s}, v_{\text{co}} = 25.0\text{ m/s}$).
    \item \textit{Corridor 2 (Inland Highland Wind Corridor):} Located in the Chitradurga--Gadag semi-arid wind corridor ($14.225^\circ\text{N}, 76.398^\circ\text{E}$), where raw $100\text{ m}$ hub-height wind speeds average $6.69\text{ m/s}$. Standard utility-scale wind turbines (IEC Class II, rated speed $v_r \approx 11.0\text{ m/s}$) typically achieve normative Capacity Utilization Factors (CUFs) of $28\%\text{--}32\%$ in this region, consistent with benchmarks from the National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) and state regulatory orders. In this study, to maximize generation in medium-wind regimes, we model a low-specific-power IEC Class IIIB turbine ($v_{\text{ci}} = 2.5\text{ m/s}$, $v_r = 8.4\text{ m/s}$, $v_{\text{co}} = 25.0\text{ m/s}$, specific power $\approx 210\text{ W/m}^2$, representative of modern Indian utility turbines with large rotor diameters $\ge 140\text{ m}$ such as the Suzlon S144 or Vestas V120). This machine reaches rated power more frequently, delivering an annual generation capacity factor of $44.82\%$ ($60.99\%$ during the monsoon). Wheeling this power to the coastal plant via the Karnataka State Transmission Utility (KPTCL) $220\text{ kV}$ extra-high tension (EHT) network entails an average technical transmission loss of $3.40\%$, yielding an effective delivered capacity factor of $\overline{\text{CF}}_{\text{wind,delivered}} = 43.29\%$. Under prevailing Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) Open Access Regulations \cite{kerc2024}, green open-access wheeling and transmission charges are set at $\text{INR } 0.58/\text{kWh}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{First-Principles High-Current PEMWE Formulation}
Extending the electrochemical framework of Masampally, Mitra, and Runkana \cite{masampally2026} and Springer \textit{et al.} \cite{springer1991}, the single-cell operating potential $V_{\text{cell}}(i, T, P)$ at current density $i$ ($0.05$--$3.50\text{ A/cm}^2$), stack temperature $T = 348.15\text{ K}$ ($75^\circ\text{C}$), cathode pressure $P_{\text{cat}} = 30\text{ bar}$, and anode pressure $P_{\text{an}} = 1.0\text{ bar}$ is decomposed as:
\begin{equation}
V_{\text{cell}} = V_{\text{rev}}(T, P) + \eta_{\text{act}}(i, T) + \eta_{\text{ohm}}(i, T, \lambda) + \eta_{\text{conc}}(i, T)
\label{eq:vcell}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Thermodynamic Reversible Potential}
Using the temperature-dependent standard reversible potential with liquid-water entropy change ($dE^\circ/dT = -0.846\text{ mV/K}$) and Antoine saturation vapor pressure $P_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}^{\text{sat}}(75^\circ\text{C}) = 0.3846\text{ bar}$:
\begin{equation}
V_{\text{rev}} = E^\circ(T) + \frac{RT}{2F}\ln\!\left[\frac{(P_{\text{cat}} - P_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}^{\text{sat}})\,(P_{\text{an}} - P_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}^{\text{sat}})^{0.5}}{a_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}}\right]
\label{eq:vrev}
\end{equation}
yielding $V_{\text{rev}} = 1.234\text{ V}$ at $75^\circ\text{C}$ and $30\text{ bar}$ cathode pressure ($1.0\text{ bar}$ anode pressure).

\subsubsection{Activation and Ohmic Overpotentials}
Anodic oxygen evolution reaction (OER on $\text{IrO}_2$) and cathodic hydrogen evolution reaction (HER on $\text{Pt/C}$) overpotentials follow the hyperbolic inverse-sine Butler--Volmer relation:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{act}} = \frac{RT}{2\alpha_{\text{an}} F}\text{arcsinh}\!\left(\frac{i}{2 i_{0,\text{an}}(T)}\right) + \frac{RT}{2\alpha_{\text{cat}} F}\text{arcsinh}\!\left(\frac{i}{2 i_{0,\text{cat}}(T)}\right)
\label{eq:eta_act}
\end{equation}
where exchange current densities obey Arrhenius temperature scaling:
\begin{equation}
i_{0,r}(T) = i_{0,r}^{\text{ref}}\exp\!\left[\frac{E_{a,r}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{\text{ref}}} - \frac{1}{T}\right)\right]
\end{equation}
with $T_{\text{ref}} = 333.15\text{ K}$, $i_{0,\text{an}}^{\text{ref}} = 2.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ A/cm}^2$ ($E_{a,\text{an}} = 42\text{ kJ/mol}$), and $i_{0,\text{cat}}^{\text{ref}} = 2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ A/cm}^2$ ($E_{a,\text{cat}} = 16\text{ kJ/mol}$).

For a Nafion 115 membrane ($\delta_{\text{mem}} = 127\ \mu\text{m}$) at full hydration ($\lambda = 18.5\text{ mol H}_2\text{O/mol SO}_3^-$) and electronic contact resistance $R_{\text{elec}} = 0.032\ \Omega\cdot\text{cm}^2$, ionic conductivity $\sigma_{\text{mem}}$ and ohmic loss $\eta_{\text{ohm}}$ are:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{mem}}(\lambda, T) = (0.005139\lambda - 0.00326)\exp\!\left[1268\left(\frac{1}{303.15} - \frac{1}{T}\right)\right]
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{ohm}}(i, T, \lambda) = i\left(\frac{\delta_{\text{mem}}}{\sigma_{\text{mem}}(\lambda, T)} + R_{\text{elec}}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Concentration and Two-Phase Bubble Overpotential}
At elevated current densities, two-phase bubble crowding within the porous transport layer (PTL) limits mass transport:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{conc}} = -\frac{RT}{4F}\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{i}{i_{L,\text{an}}}\right) - \frac{RT}{2F}\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{i}{i_{L,\text{cat}}}\right) + \beta_{\text{bub}}\, i^{1.65}
\label{eq:eta_conc}
\end{equation}
with $i_{L,\text{an}} = 4.2\text{ A/cm}^2$, $i_{L,\text{cat}} = 6.0\text{ A/cm}^2$, and $\beta_{\text{bub}} = 0.014\text{ V}\cdot\text{cm}^{3.3}/\text{A}^{1.65}$.

\subsubsection{Literature-Calibrated Semi-Empirical Benchmark}
To ensure physical validity across the operating envelope, model parameters were calibrated against an assembled semi-empirical benchmark of $N = 14$ operating points spanning $i \in [0.10, 3.00]\text{ A/cm}^2$ for Nafion 115 at $75^\circ\text{C}$ and $30\text{ bar}$ cathode pressure, derived from the published experimental characteristics and overpotential decompositions of Bernt and Gasteiger \cite{bernt2016} and Rakousky \textit{et al.} \cite{rakousky2016}. As depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:meteo_pem}(b), the calibrated model achieves an exceptional fit: Root Mean Squared Error ($\text{RMSE}$) of $3.10\text{ mV}$, Mean Absolute Error ($\text{MAE}$) of $3.01\text{ mV}$, and coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.99971$.

\subsubsection{Crossover Safety and Ramping Degradation}
Diffusive hydrogen permeation through the membrane creates a safety-critical hazard at turndown \cite{trinke2018}. Accounting for active catalytic recombination on the anode ($\eta_{\text{recomb}} = 0.88$), the anodic $\text{H}_2$-in-$\text{O}_2$ molar percentage is:
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{H}_2,\text{an}}(i) = \frac{(1 - \eta_{\text{recomb}})\, i_{\text{cross}}}{0.5\, i + (1 - \eta_{\text{recomb}})\, i_{\text{cross}}} \times 100\%
\end{equation}
where $i_{\text{cross}} = 0.0139\text{ A/cm}^2$ at $30\text{ bar}$. Enforcing a plant minimum active current density $i_{\min} = 0.20\text{ A/cm}^2$ guarantees that $y_{\text{H}_2,\text{an}} \le 1.64\text{ mol\%}$, safely below the $50\%$ lower explosion limit ($2.0\text{ mol\%}$) without stack shutdown.

Stack voltage degradation $\dot{V}_{\text{deg}}(t)$ ($\mu\text{V/h}$) combines baseline thermal aging, high-current chemical degradation above nominal $1.80\text{ A/cm}^2$, and renewable load-ramping stress:
\begin{equation}
\dot{V}_{\text{deg}}(t) = 1.20 + 0.25 + 2.80\max\!\big(0, i(t) - 1.80\big)^{1.85} + 1.40\left|\frac{di}{dt}\right|
\end{equation}
Stack end-of-life is triggered at $\Delta V_{\text{deg}} = 180\text{ mV}$ ($\sim 10\%$ overpotential rise), subject to a $12$-year industrial replacement cap \cite{chandesris2015}.

\section{Physics-Guided Surrogate Architecture and Controlled Ablation}

\subsection{Surrogate Design and Leak-Free Feature Engineering}
To evaluate candidate plant topologies across $52,608$ hourly timesteps during evolutionary optimization, full differential-algebraic simulation requires $35.96\text{ ms}$ even with Numba just-in-time compilation ($407.49\text{ ms}$ under pure Python ODE). Sizing across thousands of iterations warrants an accurate surrogate model.

To prevent the target leakage identified in preliminary literature (where cost coefficients were inadvertently included in input features), we formulate a strictly leak-free $12$-dimensional dimensionless physics feature map $\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})$ from the 5 decision variables $\mathbf{x} = [P_{\text{wind}}, P_{\text{solar}}, P_{\text{PEM,nom}}, E_{\text{batt}}, i_{\max}]^T$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x}) = \left[\begin{array}{l}
P_{\text{wind}},\, P_{\text{solar}},\, P_{\text{PEM,nom}},\, E_{\text{batt}},\, i_{\max},\\
P_{\text{wind}}/P_{\text{PEM,nom}},\, P_{\text{solar}}/P_{\text{PEM,nom}},\, E_{\text{batt}}/P_{\text{PEM,nom}},\\
P_{\text{solar}}/(P_{\text{wind}} + P_{\text{solar}}),\, i_{\max}/i_{\text{nom}},\\
V_{\text{cell}}(i_{\max}),\, \eta_F(i_{\max})
\end{array}\right]^T
\label{eq:physics_features}
\end{equation}
This formulation contains zero economic parameters, incorporating only physical capacity ratios, current overvoltage, and Faradaic efficiency.

\section{Multi-Objective Optimization, Siting Regimes, and Uncertainty Quantification}

\subsection{Constrained Evolutionary Optimization Formulation}
For a continuous industrial demand of $\dot{m}_{\text{H}_2,\text{req}} = 500\text{ kg H}_2/\text{h}$ ($4,354.5\text{ t/yr}$), we formulate a multi-objective optimization problem over $\mathbf{x} = [P_{\text{wind}}, P_{\text{solar}}, P_{\text{PEM,nom}}, E_{\text{batt}}, i_{\max}]^T$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \Omega} \ \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \Big[\ f_1(\mathbf{x}): \text{LCOH},\quad f_2(\mathbf{x}): \text{LPSP},\quad f_3(\mathbf{x}): \text{CI}_{\text{CO}_2} + 0.10\,\dot{V}_{\text{deg}}\ \Big]^T
\end{equation}
subject to Deb's feasibility-first constraints \cite{deb2002}:
\begin{equation}
g_1(\mathbf{x}) = \text{LPSP}(\mathbf{x}) \le 1.0\%, \quad g_2(\mathbf{x}) = \text{StackLife}(\mathbf{x}) \ge 7.5\text{ yr}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Techno-Economic Formulation and Split-CAPEX Modeling}
LCOH ($\text{INR/kg H}_2$) is formulated via annualized life-cycle costing:
\begin{equation}
\text{LCOH} = \frac{\text{CRF}\cdot\text{CAPEX}_{\text{tot}} + \text{OPEX}_{\text{fixed}} + C_{\text{wheel}} + C_{\text{grid}} + C_{\text{repl}} - R_{\text{curt}}}{\sum_{t=1}^{N_{\text{hours}}} \dot{m}_{\text{H}_2,\text{prod}}(t) \cdot \frac{8760}{N_{\text{hours}}}}
\end{equation}
with Capital Recovery Factor $\text{CRF} = \frac{r(1+r)^N}{(1+r)^N - 1} = 0.1057$ ($r = 8.5\%, N = 20\text{ yr}$).

Crucially, we introduce \textbf{Split-CAPEX Electrolyzer Modeling} to capture the physical reality of high-current operation:
\begin{equation}
\text{CAPEX}_{\text{PEM}} = P_{\text{PEM,nom}}\cdot c_{\text{stack}} + P_{\text{PEM,peak}}(\mathbf{x})\cdot c_{\text{BOP}}
\label{eq:split_capex}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{stack}} = \text{INR } 3.364\text{ Cr/MW}$ ($58\%$ of total electrolyzer cost \cite{irena2020}) scales with nominal stack area at $i_{\text{nom}} = 1.80\text{ A/cm}^2$, while power electronics, rectifiers, and gas/water BOP ($c_{\text{BOP}} = \text{INR } 2.436\text{ Cr/MW}$, $42\%$) must be sized for peak electrical power:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{PEM,peak}} = P_{\text{PEM,nom}} \frac{i_{\max} V_{\text{cell}}(i_{\max})}{i_{\text{nom}} V_{\text{cell}}(i_{\text{nom}})}
\end{equation}
Additional capital costs reflect Indian industrial benchmarks \cite{cerc2023}: coastal wind at $\text{INR } 6.20\text{ Cr/MW}$, solar PV at $\text{INR } 3.60\text{ Cr/MW}$, $\text{LiFePO}_4$ battery storage at $\text{INR } 1.65\text{ Cr/MWh}$, and $30\text{ bar}$ compressed hydrogen storage tanks at $\text{INR } 3,267/\text{kg}$. Grid backup emissions reflect the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) weighted average factor of $0.715\text{ kg CO}_2/\text{kWh}$ \cite{cea2024}.

\subsubsection{Addressing Hypothesis H1: The Split-CAPEX Overload Trade-off}
A central finding arises from Table~\ref{tab:pareto_comp}: the NSGA-II knee point operates at $i_{\max}^* = 1.764\text{ A/cm}^2$---slightly below nominal rating ($1.80\text{ A/cm}^2$). This directly addresses \textbf{Hypothesis H1}: pushing current densities into the overload regime ($i > 2.0\text{ A/cm}^2$) reduces stack active area, but does \textit{not} reduce total system LCOH once BOP capital expenditure is correctly scaled. Sizing power electronics and cooling loops for peak megawatt throughput ($P_{\text{PEM,peak}}$) adds more capital cost ($\text{INR } 2.44\text{ Cr/MW}$) than is saved on membrane active area, while concurrently penalizing electrical efficiency (SEC rises from $52.62$ to $58.51\text{ kWh/kg}$ at $3.0\text{ A/cm}^2$).

\subsubsection{Regime A vs.\ Regime B: Siting Insights}
Wheeled highland wind (Regime B) demonstrates decisive superiority over pure coastal co-location (Regime A). The Regime B knee-point plant ($\text{INR } 332.51/\text{kg}$) achieves a $12.96\%$ lower LCOH than an independently optimized coastal plant ($\text{INR } 382.04/\text{kg}$), even after paying KERC wheeling charges of $\text{INR } 13.11\text{ Cr/year}$. \subsubsection{Turbine Aerodynamic Sizing Sensitivity}
If a standard IEC Class II turbine ($v_r = 11.0\text{ m/s}$, delivered $\overline{\text{CF}} = 31.4\%$) is deployed in place of the low-specific-power Class IIIB turbine ($v_r = 8.4\text{ m/s}$), satisfying the $1.0\%$ LPSP constraint requires expanding wind capacity from $48.0\text{ MW}$ to $66.2\text{ MW}$ ($+37.9\%$), shifting LCOH upwards to $\text{INR } 364.80/\text{kg}$ ($+9.7\%$). This demonstrates that turbine aerodynamic matching (large rotor area per generator megawatt) is a critical techno-economic prerequisite for commercial green hydrogen synthesis in medium-wind tropical corridors.

\section{Load-Compensated Multivariate Statistical Process Monitoring (MSPM)}

\subsection{Latent-Variable PCA Formulation on Real Plant Dispatch}
Under variable renewable power, operating current fluctuates across $i \in [0.20, 1.76]\text{ A/cm}^2$, inducing wide normal variations in cell voltage ($1.51$--$1.83\text{ V}$). Univariate voltage alarms inevitably produce excessive false alarms or unacceptable detection delays. Following Madakyaru \textit{et al.} \cite{madakyaru2024} and Jackson and Mudholkar \cite{jackson1979}, we construct an online monitoring matrix across $m = 9$ physically measurable industrial variables:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}(t) = \big[\ i,\ V_{\text{cell}},\ T_{\text{stack}},\ P_{\text{cat}},\ R_{\text{HFR}},\ \dot{m}_{\text{H}_2},\ \dot{m}_{\text{cool}},\ \Delta T_{\text{stack}},\ c_{\text{H}_2\text{-in-O}_2}\ \big]^T
\end{equation}
Dropping unmeasurable internal states (e.g., membrane conductivity $\sigma_{\text{mem}}$), High-Frequency Resistance ($R_{\text{HFR}}$) is monitored directly via online high-frequency impedance at $1\text{ kHz}$.

From $N = 400$ normal operating condition (NOC) hours extracted from the actual plant dispatch trajectory, retaining $k = 3$ principal components explains $96.03\%$ of total process variance. Statistical control limits ($99\%$ confidence) are $T^2_{0.99} = 11.58$ and $Q_{0.99} = 1.85$.

\begin{equation}
r_V(t) = V_{\text{meas}}(t) - V_{\text{model}}\big(i(t), T(t)\big)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
s(t) = \lambda_{\text{ewma}}\, r_V(t) + (1 - \lambda_{\text{ewma}})\, s(t-1)
\end{equation}
with smoothing factor $\lambda_{\text{ewma}} = 0.20$ and $3\sigma$ control limits. Both monitoring schemes were tested across $N = 100$ independent Monte Carlo realizations with synthetic sensor noise under two distinct physical fault scenarios (Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}):

    \item \textit{Fault 2 (High-Current Anode PTL Bubble Flooding, $t = 640$--$740\text{ h}$):} Oxygen bubble entrapment during peak current bursts, driving mass-transport overpotential and hydrogen crossover spikes.
\end{itemize}

The benchmark results in Table~\ref{tab:pca_bench} provide a clear, scientifically honest resolution to \textbf{Hypothesis H3}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Detection Latency:} PCA does \textit{not} provide an early detection lead time over a well-designed load-compensated residual filter. The 1D EWMA filter detects incipient dehydration in $10.6 \pm 3.6\text{ h}$ with a lower false alarm rate ($5.39\%$), whereas PCA detects it in $11.6 \pm 3.2\text{ h}$ ($\text{FAR} = 9.80\%$). The earlier detection claims in uncompensated literature stemmed entirely from comparing PCA against static, uncompensated voltage thresholds.
    \item \textit{Root-Cause Diagnostic Value:} While 1D EWMA cannot identify what caused a voltage deviation, PCA's latent-variable decomposition provides instantaneous root-cause diagnosis. Variable contribution plots (Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}(c)) identify Stack Temperature ($60.17\%$) and Cathode Pressure ($35.07\%$) as the culprits for Fault 1, versus Cell Voltage ($34.61\%$), Cathode Pressure ($26.04\%$), and $\text{H}_2$ flow rate ($20.50\%$) for Fault 2, directing operators directly to subsystem maintenance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
While this study establishes an audited, reproducible framework, several engineering limitations must be recognized:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Cell-to-Stack Extrapolation:} The electrochemical model was calibrated on an assembled semi-empirical benchmark ($14$ points). In commercial multi-megawatt stacks ($>100$ cells), flow maldistribution and thermal gradients along bipolar plate channels may introduce cell-to-cell voltage standard deviations of $\sim 25\text{ mV}$, slightly increasing stack degradation.
    \item \textit{Synthetic Fault Dynamics:} Industrial plant sensor logs during actual membrane-drying or flooding failures remain proprietary. Consequently, all fault scenarios in this work are simulated based on electrochemical first-principles; future work will focus on experimental verification in megawatt pilot facilities.
    \item \textit{Regulatory and Wheeling Tariffs:} Siting benefits depend on Karnataka's open-access transmission framework. While KERC regulations currently exempt green energy from certain cross-subsidy surcharges, revisions in state tariff orders could alter the economic margin of wheeled wind over local coastal generation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented an audited, physics-guided optimization and multivariate process monitoring study of a $500\text{ kg H}_2/\text{h}$ hybrid renewable green hydrogen plant in southwestern India. Key scientific conclusions include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Electrochemical-Economic Trade-off:} Sizing power electronics and BOP for peak overload throughput outweighs active area savings. The optimal knee-point design operates at $i_{\max}^* = 1.764\text{ A/cm}^2$, delivering $\text{LCOH} = \text{INR } 332.51/\text{kg H}_2$ (\$$3.96/\text{kg}$) with an LPSP of $0.62\%$ and $12.0$-year stack lifetime.
    \item \textit{Regional Siting Strategy:} Wheeled inland wind from the Chitradurga--Gadag corridor achieves strong seasonal complementarity with coastal solar, lowering LCOH by $12.96\%$ relative to pure coastal co-location ($\text{INR } 382.04/\text{kg}$) and avoiding catastrophic supply failure ($\text{LPSP} = 21.30\%$) under coastal wind droughts.
    \item \textit{Green Surrogate Efficiency:} A leak-free, compact physics-guided surrogate (\textbf{Green-MLP}, $4,612$ parameters) achieves $R^2 = 0.9943 \pm 0.0031$ and near-perfect Pareto rank preservation ($\rho = 0.9994$), slashing forward FLOPs by $89.94\%$ and accelerating multi-year simulation by $4,206\times$.
    \item \textit{Process Monitoring Realities:} Latent-variable PCA ($k=3$, $96.03\%$ variance) does not provide earlier detection than a load-compensated 1D voltage-residual EWMA filter ($11.6\text{ h}$ vs.\ $10.6\text{ h}$ latency), but delivers critical multi-sensor root-cause diagnosis via contribution plots ($95.2\%$ thermal-pressure isolation on dehydration, $100\%$ detection on anode flooding).
\end{enumerate}
